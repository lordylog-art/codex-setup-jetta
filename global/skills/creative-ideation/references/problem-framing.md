# Problem Framing

Use this reference when the user's request is vague, broad, overloaded, or missing the strategic center.

## Core Reframe Template

Translate the request into:

`Need -> Audience -> Desired change -> Constraints -> Success signal`

Example:

- raw ask: "give me app ideas"
- framed ask: "create app concepts for busy professionals that save time weekly, can be tested cheaply, and have clear paid value"

## Framing Questions To Answer Internally

- What is the user really trying to improve, unlock, or avoid?
- Who feels this pain most sharply?
- What makes this request hard: saturation, execution cost, trust, novelty, or clarity?
- What type of output is most useful now: volume, contrast, prioritization, or build-ready concepts?
- Is the goal exploration, selection, or execution?

## Useful Reframes

### Turn category requests into opportunity requests

Avoid:

- "podcast ideas"

Prefer:

- "podcast concepts for founders who want tactical insights without long interviews"

### Turn format requests into outcome requests

Avoid:

- "marketing campaign ideas"

Prefer:

- "campaign concepts that make a technical product feel simple and urgent for first-time buyers"

### Turn generic solution requests into strategic bets

Avoid:

- "business ideas"

Prefer:

- "small business concepts with low fixed cost, local trust advantage, and recurring revenue potential"

## Constraint Levers

If ideas are too generic, reframe using one or more levers:

- tighter audience
- stronger emotional outcome
- distribution-first thinking
- low-budget launch
- premium positioning
- time-limited execution
- strong personal edge or unfair advantage
- channel constraint
- regulation or trust constraint

## Assumption Handling

When details are missing, state assumptions in one short line and proceed. Do not turn a lightweight ideation task into an intake interview.
